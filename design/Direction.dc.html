Ex Libris · phase 0 · direction

Two decisions to make, and the system they sit in

The serif and the figure are yours to pick. Everything below them — palette, scale, motion — is built and will not change unless you want it to. Page chrome here is set in the interface face, not in any of the three candidates, so nothing is weighted.

Each column shows the wordmark, a book card at real size, and an axis line. The axis line is the hardest test — it is the one place the serif carries meaning rather than decoration.

Interface face — IBM Plex Sans, proposed

The brief allowed Inter or an alternative with proper tabular figures. The frontend-design skill names Inter as a face to avoid, and it carries the exact cliché §3.6 is steering away from. Plex is humanist rather than neutral, variable, free to self-host, and slightly technical in a way that suits the subject matter.

4 · Motion — three real springs

Encoded as linear(), not ease-in-out. Tap a row to run it. The difference is small on a bar and large on a phone.

A photograph that overshoots reads as wobble, not as spring — which is why the cover flight gets the flattest of the three, and the FAB gets the bounciest.

5 · The recurring figure — two directions

Both directions are monoline, one weight, one ink, and both appear five to eight times in the whole app and never on a screen that has books on it. One caveat before you look: SVG figure drawing is the weakest thing I do. Ornaments, frames and rules will be good. A figure will be a competent diagram of a figure, and one of these two is beyond that — it says so on its card rather than pretending otherwise.

A · the crow

A watcher, not a companion. Sits on an empty state's rule and looks at nothing. Reads well against ruthless protagonists and hard magic, and it survives being drawn very small — a crow at 24px is still a crow.

Risk: crows are common shorthand for dark fiction. It could read as on-the-nose.

B · the hands
not drawn —
see below

The act rather than a character. Holding, turning, marking a place — three or four variations of the same pair of hands, one per empty state. Nothing to anthropomorphise, so it can never turn into a mascot.

There is no sketch because I could not draw one worth judging. Three attempts read as a scroll, a book on a stand, and a spiral notebook. The crow is within what I can draw; hands are not. If you choose B you are choosing the idea, and the line art gets drawn by a person or sourced in Phase 5 — I would build the system around it either way.

Recommendation

The hands are the better idea and the crow is the only one I can deliver. The hands describe the act the app is about; the crow decorates the genre. So the choice is really between a good idea that needs someone else's hand and a serviceable one I can execute now. If you want the app finished entirely from this session, take the crow. If you are willing to commission or source six small drawings, take the hands and I will design every empty state around them. The frame ornament is the same either way and is settled.
